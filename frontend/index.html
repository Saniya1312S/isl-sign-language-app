📌 Section A: Project Overview

Indian Sign Language Alphabet Recognition and Multilingual Communication

This project uses computer vision and machine learning to recognize static Indian Sign Language alphabet gestures from A to Z. The recognized letters are combined to form English words and sentences. The completed sentence can then be translated into Hindi or Marathi and sent to a Gemini AI assistant for further interaction.

⚠️ Scope Clarification: The current recognition model recognizes static alphabet signs (fingerspelling A–Z), not independent word-level gestures. Full words and sentences are composed through sequential fingerspelling.

5-Step Inference Pipeline:

1
Capture: Live webcam frame or uploaded image is ingested and decoded into OpenCV BGR color format.
2
Hand Tracking: MediaPipe Hands detects up to 2 hands (max_num_hands=2) and extracts 21 3D spatial landmarks $(x, y, z)$.
3
Normalization & Feature Extraction: Hands are canonicalized left-to-right by wrist-$x$ coordinate. A 171-dimensional invariant feature vector is computed (wrist-centered coordinates, inter-fingertip distances, and inter-wrist vectors).
4
Classification: The trained SVM model evaluates the 171-d vector and outputs the predicted letter (A–Z) and genuine probability confidence.
5
Display & Composition: The letter is displayed alongside the MediaPipe landmark skeleton overlay and fed into the continuous sentence accumulator.

🧭 Section G: Honest Limitations and Future Scope

✅ Current Implemented Scope:

  • Static A–Z Indian Sign Language alphabet fingerspelling.
  • Dual-hand landmark extraction (1-hand and 2-hand ISL alphabet support).
  • 5-second stability detection to filter out transitioning hand positions.
  • Sequential letter composition forming English words and sentences.
  • Instant dictionary and phonetic Devanagari translation into Hindi and Marathi.
  • Speech pronunciation using client browser Web Speech API.
  • Optional conversational AI assistance using Google Gemini.

🚀 Future Development Scope:

  • Continuous dynamic sign gesture recognition across time using temporal models (e.g. GRU / LSTM / Transformer).
  • Independent word-level and phrase-level ISL gesture vocabulary.
  • Expanded training datasets featuring varied skin tones, backgrounds, and non-uniform lighting.
  • Cross-regional Indian sign dialects and additional state languages (e.g. Tamil, Telugu, Bengali).
  • Mobile-native camera optimization and edge inference (ONNX / TFLite) for low-power offline smartphones.
